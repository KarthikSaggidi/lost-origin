Coffee Shop Software: Product and Technical Specification
Nov 10, 2026 · @Karthik Saggidi
This document specifies a responsive take-away-only coffee shop website with online ordering and pickleball court booking, plus a complete admin panel covering inventory, POS, payments, accounting, WhatsApp and partner management.
1. Overview and goals
The business is a take-away-only coffee shop with a pickleball court, run by three or more partners. The software must let customers order and book online, let staff bill at the counter, and let partners see stock, cash, tax and profit in one place.
Business goals
• Take 30% or more of orders online within six months, reducing counter queues.
• Fill off-peak court hours through online booking, advance payment and offers.
• Give every partner a trusted, auditable view of sales, expenses and profit share.
• Cut stock wastage and stock-outs through recipe-based inventory.
In scope
• Public responsive website: hero, banners, menu, take-away ordering, court booking, contact.
• Admin panel: dashboard, orders, POS and offline billing, inventory, payments, pickleball bookings, accounting, partners, campaigns and banners, WhatsApp, reports, settings.
• Integrations: payment gateway, WhatsApp Business API, SMS/email, thermal printers.
Out of scope (version 1)
• Home delivery, delivery partners and delivery-zone logic. Orders are pickup only.
• Native mobile apps. The website is built as an installable progressive web app (PWA) instead.
• Multi-branch support. The data model is branch-ready, but only one outlet is launched.
Key rules that shape the design
1. Every order is take-away. The customer chooses a pickup time slot, never an address.
2. Online orders and counter bills share one order table, one stock ledger and one payment ledger.
3. Court bookings and cafe sales feed the same accounting books but are reported as separate revenue streams.
4. Money-related records are never deleted. Corrections are made through reversing entries.

Rules
• Permissions are configurable per role at the module level: view, create, edit, delete, approve, export.
• Partners can be required to approve high-value items, for example expenses above a set amount or any capital withdrawal.
• Staff log in with a username and PIN at the counter, and with email and password plus optional two-step verification on the admin panel.
• Customers log in with a mobile number and a one-time password (OTP). Guest checkout is allowed for take-away orders.
3. System architecture
The website, admin panel and POS all call the same backend, so an online order, a counter bill and a court booking land in the same stock, payment and accounting records.
4. Customer website
The website is mobile-first, loads in under three seconds on a 4G phone, and every visible block (hero, banners, menu, offers, opening hours, court prices) is editable from the admin panel without a developer.

5. Take-away ordering
Every online order ends with the customer collecting at the counter. There is no address field, no delivery charge and no rider logic anywhere in the product.
Customer flow
1. Customer browses the menu and adds items with size, add-ons and notes.
2. At checkout the customer enters name and mobile number, verified by OTP for first-time guests.
3. Customer picks a pickup slot, for example today 5:15 to 5:30 pm. Slots are capped by how many drinks the bar can make per 15 minutes.
4. Customer pays online (UPI, card, wallet) or chooses pay at counter, if the admin has enabled it for that customer type.
5. The order appears on the POS and kitchen screen with a sound alert, and the customer receives a WhatsApp confirmation with a pickup code.
6. When the order is ready the customer gets a ready message, shows the code at the counter, and the cashier marks it collected.

Ordering rules (all editable in settings)
• Opening and closing time, last-order time, and a pause switch for when the shop is too busy.
• Minimum preparation time, for example 10 minutes, so the first slot is never too soon.
• Maximum orders per slot, and a maximum drinks-per-slot limit for the bar.
• Advance ordering up to a set number of days, for example 2.
• Online cancellation allowed until the order moves to Preparing. After that, only a manager can cancel.
• No-show policy: prepaid food orders are not refunded after a set grace time, for example 30 minutes, unless the manager overrides.
• Stock-aware menu: an item is hidden or marked sold out automatically when its ingredients run out (see Inventory).
• Reorder button and saved favourites for repeat customers.
Kitchen display screen
A full-screen board with columns New, Preparing, Ready. Late orders turn amber and then red. Each ticket shows pickup time, items with modifiers, notes and source (online, counter). Tapping a ticket moves it forward.
6. Pickleball court booking
Court booking is a separate module with its own calendar, pricing and reports, but the same customer account, payment gateway and accounting books as the cafe.
Customer flow
1. Customer opens Pickleball, picks a date, and sees a grid of courts against time slots with free, booked and blocked states.
2. Customer selects one or more consecutive slots, adds the number of players, and sees the price including tax.
3. Customer pays the full amount or an advance (for example 50%) online. Unpaid holds expire after 10 minutes.
4. WhatsApp confirmation with court, time, and a QR code for check-in. Reminder 2 hours before.
5. On arrival, staff scan the QR code. The booking becomes Checked in, and the customer can add cafe items to the same bill.
Court setup (admin)
• Courts: name, indoor or outdoor, surface, status, photos.
• Slot length: 30, 60 or 90 minutes, set per court.
• Operating hours per weekday, with holiday and maintenance blocks.
• Equipment as add-ons: paddle rental, ball pack, coaching session.

Booking rules
• No double booking. The system locks a slot at the database level while a payment is in progress.
• Cancellation window, for example full refund up to 12 hours before, 50% up to 4 hours, none after.
• Rescheduling allowed once, subject to availability.

8. Inventory
Inventory is recipe-based: every sale automatically deducts raw materials, so stock on screen matches stock on the shelf apart from wastage and theft, which are tracked separately.
Item types
• Raw materials: coffee beans, milk, syrups, sugar, cups, lids, sleeves, bakery inputs.
• Finished goods bought ready: packaged snacks, bottled water, bakery items.
• Prepared items: house-made sauces or batters with their own recipe and yield.
• Court consumables: balls, grip tapes, rental paddles (tracked as assets with a count).

9. POS and offline billing
The point-of-sale screen is a touch-first web app that runs on a counter tablet or PC. It keeps billing even when the internet drops, and syncs when the connection returns.
Billing features
• Quick product grid by category, search, barcode scan, and favourites for the fastest sellers.
• Modifiers (size, milk type, extra shot), item notes and combo handling.
• Order source tag: counter, online pickup, court booking add-on, phone order.
• Discounts by percentage or amount with a reason, protected by a manager PIN above a limit.
• Split payment across cash, UPI and card, and a split bill by items or by person.
• Hold and recall bills, merge or move items between bills, and a repeat-last-order button for regulars.
• Customer lookup by mobile number to apply loyalty points and send the bill by WhatsApp.
• Court add-on: attach cafe items to a court booking and settle everything at checkout.
Receipts and printing
• GST-compliant tax invoice with shop name, GSTIN, FSSAI number, invoice number, HSN or SAC codes, tax breakup and a payment QR code.
• Thermal printing on 58 mm or 80 mm printers, kitchen order ticket (KOT) printing for the bar, and PDF or WhatsApp receipt as an option.
• Gapless invoice numbering per financial year, as GST requires. Cancelled bills are voided with a reason and a credit note, never deleted.

Shifts and cash control
1. Cashier opens a shift with an opening cash count.
2. All cash in, cash out (petty cash, supplier payments) and refunds are logged against the shift.
3. At close, the cashier counts cash by denomination. The system shows expected cash, actual cash and the difference.
4. A day-end report (Z-report) is generated with sales by payment mode, tax collected, discounts, voids and refunds, and then sent to managers and partners.
Hardware supported: thermal receipt printer, cash drawer, barcode scanner, customer-facing display (optional), card or UPI soundbox (optional).

Payment gateway
A single India-based gateway such as Razorpay, Cashfree or PayU is integrated through a payment-provider layer, so the gateway can be swapped without changing the order logic. The final choice depends on fees and settlement terms, listed in the open questions.
Rules the system enforces
• Payment status is confirmed only by the gateway webhook, never by the customer's browser, so a closed tab cannot create a false paid order.
• Duplicate-payment protection through idempotency keys on every payment request.
• Held orders and court slots are released automatically if payment is not completed in 10 minutes.
• Refunds go back to the original payment source, are linked to the order or booking, and need manager approval above a set amount.
• Partial refunds, for example one item missing, are supported, with a credit note generated automatically.
• Card and UPI details are never stored on the shop's servers. The gateway holds them.
Reconciliation
• Daily import of gateway settlement reports and matching against the ledger, with unmatched or short-settled payments flagged.
• Gateway fees and GST on fees are booked automatically as expenses.
• A cash book and a bank book, with deposit entries for cash taken to the bank.
• Payment reports: collection by mode, failed payments, refunds, pending settlements, chargebacks.
11. Accounting and partner management
With three or more partners, the books must be transparent and auditable. The accounting module uses double-entry bookkeeping and posts entries automatically from sales, purchases, payments and stock, so partners do not depend on spreadsheets. Tax rates and rules below are configurable; the exact treatment (regular GST or composition, rates for food versus court rental) must be confirmed with the shop's chartered accountant.

Partner management
• Partner register: name, share percentage, capital contributed, date joined, bank details.
• Capital account per partner: contributions, drawings, loans to the business, and profit credited.
• Profit-sharing engine: define share percentages and optional rules, for example salaried partner, interest on capital, or different shares for the cafe and the court. The system calculates each partner's share every month.
• Drawings and withdrawals require approval by a set number of other partners, with a full trail.
• Partner statement: a PDF per partner showing opening balance, contributions, withdrawals, profit share and closing balance.
• Approval workflows: expenses above a limit, new supplier, price changes, large refunds, and stock write-offs can need approval from one or two partners through the panel or WhatsApp.
• Entry or exit of a partner is handled as a dated event, so history remains correct.
Financial reports
• Profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow, trial balance, and day book.
• Profit by revenue stream: cafe counter, online orders, court bookings, equipment rental.
• Cost of goods sold and gross margin by product category.
• Budget against actual per month, and break-even view.
• Scheduled monthly partner report by email and WhatsApp.
• Export to Excel, PDF and Tally-compatible format for the external auditor.

Design rules
• Stock changes only through stock_ledger; the current quantity is a computed summary that is verified nightly.
• Accounting changes only through journal_entries with balanced debit and credit lines; closed periods are locked.
• Invoices, payments and journal entries are append-only. A mistake is corrected by a credit note or a reversing entry.
• A unique constraint on court, date and slot prevents double booking even under simultaneous requests.
• Soft delete (an archived flag) for products, customers and staff, so history stays intact.
• Indexes on order status and time, booking date and court, and customer mobile number for fast lookups.
• Daily automated backups with a tested restore, covered in Security.

• SMS provider for OTP fallback (DLT-registered sender in India).
• Email service (for example Amazon SES) for invoices and reports.
• Google Maps for the location block, Google Analytics and Meta Pixel for marketing measurement.
• Object storage and a CDN for images and videos.
• Error monitoring (Sentry) and uptime monitoring.
API standards: JSON, consistent error codes, pagination on all lists, rate limiting, idempotency keys on payment and order creation, and an OpenAPI document generated automatically for developers.
16. UI/UX and responsive design
The customer site feels warm and quick, with large food photography. The admin panel is dense and fast, built for daily work rather than decoration. Both share one design system so new screens look consistent.
Design system
• Colours: a warm coffee palette (espresso brown, cream, one accent such as terracotta or green for the court), with status colours for success, warning and error. Final colours come from the brand logo.
• Type: one display font for headings and one clean sans-serif for body and numbers; minimum 16 px body text on mobile.
• Components: buttons, cards, product tiles, slot chips, modals, toasts, data tables, filters, charts, empty and loading states, all in light mode and an optional dark mode for the admin panel.
• Imagery: compressed WebP or AVIF images, lazy loading, and a fixed aspect ratio per placement so uploads never break layouts.

17. Security, backup and compliance
The system holds customer phone numbers, payments and the partners' financial data, so security is designed in from the start.
Application security
• HTTPS everywhere, secure cookies, and modern password hashing (Argon2 or bcrypt).
• Role-based access on every API call, checked on the server and not only hidden in the screen.
• Optional two-step verification for admin, partner and accountant accounts, and mandatory for Super Admin.
• Protection against common attacks: SQL injection, cross-site scripting, request forgery, brute-force login, and file-upload abuse. Rate limits on OTP and login.
• Payment webhooks verified by signature. Card and UPI data never touch the shop's servers.
• Audit log for logins, price changes, refunds, discounts, stock adjustments, role changes and data exports.
• Secrets (gateway keys, WhatsApp tokens) kept in an encrypted secret store, never in code.
